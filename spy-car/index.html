Where You Left Off

You have just completed your first prototype of a laser-cut walking robot — a plywood body powered by a hobby gearbox, with front and back legs driven by a crank-and-transmission mechanism. It was assembled from 3 mm plywood panels that slot together like puzzle pieces.

💡
First-time prototypes are never perfect — and that is the point. Maybe your robot wobbles. Maybe the legs jam. Maybe the battery holder falls out during testing. Every one of these observations is valuable engineering data. In this activity you'll use the engineering design process to analyse what went wrong and plan a targeted improvement.

Watch For…
Watch for…Why it matters for YOUR robot
Defining the problem clearlyWhat is the specific, measurable problem with your robot? (wobble, leg jamming, battery falling out, slow speed?)
Criteria and constraintsWhat must your robot do (criteria)? What can you NOT change — same motor, same materials (constraints)?
Brainstorming multiple ideasEngineers always generate several options before choosing one. How many ideas can you come up with for your problem?
Testing and measuring resultsHow will you prove your improvement actually made things better? What will you measure?
Iterating — improve, don't scrapGood engineers tweak and refine. You are not starting from scratch; you are improving what you already built.

1️⃣ Observe Your Prototype

💡 Example: "The back legs dragged on the ground instead of lifting, so the robot barely moved forward."

2️⃣ Root Cause Analysis

💡 Think about: leg geometry, joint friction, motor torque, weight distribution, crank offset…

3️⃣ Criteria & Constraints

💡 Criteria: "must walk in a straight line for 30 cm." Constraint: "must use the existing gearbox and 2× AA batteries."

4️⃣ Brainstorm Solutions

💡 Longer legs? Different leg angle? Added weights? Rubber feet? Different crank position?

5️⃣ Select Your Best Idea

💡 Which directly addresses the root cause? Which can be tested quickly in one lesson?

6️⃣ Plan Your Test

💡 Be specific: "I'll measure distance walked in 10 seconds" beats "I'll see if it walks better."

Reference — Optional

Extension Activities

For students who want to extend their thinking beyond the core activity.

🤖

Extension A — Compare Real Robots

Research Boston Dynamics Spot or Honda ASIMO. How do their leg geometries compare to your robot? How do they solve balance and friction? Write a five-point comparison in your logbook.

📋

Extension B — Engineering Change Order

Engineers keep detailed lab notebooks. Photograph your current robot, annotate the image with the problems you found, then write a brief Engineering Change Order explaining each planned modification and why.

🔢

Extension C — Gearbox Maths

The hobby gearbox reduces speed and multiplies torque. Research a typical yellow gearbox gear ratio. Calculate the output torque and RPM. Does your robot need more torque or more speed to walk better?
